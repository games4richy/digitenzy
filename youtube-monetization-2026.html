YouTube Monetization in 2026: Partner Program Requirements, RPM by Niche, and Income Beyond AdSense

By Marcus Reid8 min readPublished September 14, 2026
Earnings Disclaimer Income figures in this article are based on publicly reported data and industry benchmarks, not guaranteed results. Earnings vary widely by niche, audience location, content quality, and effort. Nothing on this page is financial advice.

Almost every new YouTuber hits the same wall. You publish consistently for months, the subscriber count creeps upward, and you start wondering when the channel will actually pay. Then you look up the requirements, find conflicting numbers from different years, and realize most guides online are already out of date.

2026 makes this more confusing than usual. YouTube has lowered the bar for some earning features while announcing that the main ad revenue threshold will double for new applicants in February 2027. This guide sets out the current rules, what changes next year, what creators realistically earn by niche, and the income streams that often matter more than ads.

YouTube Partner Program Requirements Right Now

The YouTube Partner Program (YPP) currently has two entry levels. The lower level gives access to fan funding and shopping features. The higher level adds a share of advertising and YouTube Premium revenue.

YPP Eligibility (Until January 31, 2027)

Fan funding tier: subscribers500
Fan funding tier: watch hours (12 months) or Shorts views (90 days)3,000 hrs or 3M
Ad revenue tier: subscribers1,000
Ad revenue tier: watch hours (12 months) or Shorts views (90 days)4,000 hrs or 10M
Creator share of long-form ad revenue55%
Creator share of allocated Shorts revenue45%

Beyond the numbers, you need to live in a country where YPP is available, have two-step verification turned on, link an AdSense account for payments, and have no active Community Guidelines strikes. Your channel also goes through a review to check that it follows YouTube's monetization policies. Only public watch hours count, so views on unlisted, private, or deleted videos don't help you qualify.

The February 2027 Changes You Need to Plan For

In August 2026, YouTube announced significant updates to the Partner Program that take effect on February 1, 2027. According to YouTube's help center, the main changes are:

  • Higher entry threshold for ad revenue. New applicants will need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.
  • Existing members keep their place. Channels already in YPP are not affected by the higher entry requirement.
  • Fan funding and shopping thresholds stay the same. The 500 subscriber entry level isn't changing.
  • A new bar for Shorts revenue. To earn ads and subscription revenue from Shorts, channels will need 10 million qualified Shorts views over the previous 90 days. Channels below that still earn from long-form videos, and Shorts earnings resume once they cross the threshold again.
  • Activity requirements. Channels stay active by meeting at least one of these: 1,000 qualified watch hours in the past year, 1 million Shorts views in 90 days, or uploading two long-form videos or five Shorts every 90 days.
  • Premium revenue pools. Creators will share a pool worth 30% of net YouTube Premium subscription revenue and 60% of net Premium Lite revenue.

YouTube also says it is adding incentive programs, including YouTube Shopping bonuses, brand deal incentives, and earnings boosts for starting and growing trends, aimed at channels below the Shorts revenue threshold.

If you're close to qualifying, the deadline matters. A channel that reaches 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours and applies before February 1, 2027 is judged on today's rules. After that date, the same channel would need twice the watch time. If you're within reach, it's worth focusing your publishing on watch time over the next few months.

RPM vs CPM and What Different Niches Earn

Two numbers get mixed up constantly. CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is what you actually earn per 1,000 video views, after YouTube's share and accounting for views that showed no ads. RPM is always lower than CPM, and it's the number that matters for planning.

Niche has the biggest effect on RPM, because advertisers pay more to reach people who are about to make valuable decisions. Creator-reported figures, including those collected by Stan, commonly fall into these rough bands for long-form videos:

  • Finance, investing, business, and B2B software: often $10 to $30
  • Technology, education, and career topics: often $5 to $15
  • Lifestyle, food, travel, and fitness: often $3 to $10
  • Gaming, entertainment, and general vlogs: often $1 to $5

Treat these as guides, not promises. Audience location has a huge effect, since viewers in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia command higher ad rates than viewers in many other regions. RPM also rises in the fourth quarter as holiday ad spending peaks and usually drops in January. Longer videos (over eight minutes) allow mid-roll ads, which can raise earnings per view when viewers stay engaged.

To put it in perspective, a finance channel earning a $15 RPM makes about $1,500 from 100,000 monthly views. A gaming channel at a $3 RPM would need around 500,000 monthly views to earn the same.

Shorts Monetization: The Honest Math

Shorts can grow a channel quickly, but they pay much less per view than long-form videos. Ads play between Shorts in the feed rather than inside a specific video, so YouTube pools that revenue, allocates part of it to creators based on their share of Shorts views, and pays creators 45% of their allocation. Shorts RPMs are commonly reported in the range of a few cents per 1,000 views, and rarely more than a small fraction of long-form earnings.

That doesn't make Shorts pointless. They're one of the best ways to get discovered, and viewers who find you through Shorts can go on to watch your longer videos, join memberships, or buy through your links. The 2027 changes make this strategy even more important for smaller channels, since Shorts ad revenue will only be paid to channels with 10 million Shorts views every 90 days. Most creators should treat Shorts as a discovery tool and earn primarily from long-form content and other income streams.

Revenue Beyond AdSense

Successful creators rarely rely on ad revenue alone. It's the easiest income to start earning, but it's also the least predictable. These streams often grow into larger earners:

YouTube Shopping affiliate program

YouTube opened its Shopping affiliate program to Partner Program channels with 500 or more subscribers in eligible countries, including the US. You can tag products from participating brands in long-form videos, Shorts, and live streams, and earn a commission on sales. Review and tutorial channels are especially well suited to it, because viewers are already comparing products.

Channel memberships and fan funding

Memberships let viewers pay monthly for perks such as badges, members-only videos, or early access. Super Thanks, Super Chat, and Super Stickers let viewers tip on videos and live streams. These features become available at the 500 subscriber level, and a relatively small group of loyal fans can bring in steady monthly income.

Sponsorships

Brand deals often pay far more than ads for small and mid-sized channels with a focused audience. A channel with 20,000 subscribers in a specific professional niche can be more valuable to the right sponsor than a general entertainment channel ten times the size. YouTube's BrandConnect program helps connect creators and brands, though most sponsorships still come through direct outreach. Always disclose paid promotions using YouTube's paid promotion setting and a verbal or on-screen mention.

Your own products and services

Courses, templates, coaching, consulting, and physical merchandise give you the highest margin and the most control. A channel that teaches a skill can often earn more from a single digital product than from a year of ad revenue. Our guide to creating and selling a digital product walks through the process.

Affiliate links outside YouTube Shopping

Links in video descriptions to software, equipment, and books through programs like Amazon Associates or SaaS affiliate programs add another layer. Disclose them clearly, and only recommend products you'd stand behind. More on that in our affiliate marketing guide.

Keeping Your Channel Monetized

Getting accepted into YPP is only the first step. Channels can lose monetization on individual videos, or entirely, if they break YouTube's rules.

  • Follow the advertiser-friendly content guidelines. Videos with strong profanity, graphic content, or sensitive topics handled carelessly may earn limited or no ads (the yellow icon).
  • Avoid mass-produced or repetitive content. YouTube's inauthentic content policy targets channels that upload near-identical videos at scale or reuse other people's content without adding meaningful commentary or value.
  • Respect copyright. Using music, clips, or footage you don't have rights to can send revenue to the rights holder or lead to strikes.
  • Disclose altered or synthetic content. Realistic content made with AI tools must be labeled using YouTube's disclosure setting.
  • Stay active. Under the 2027 rules, channels that stop uploading and fall below the activity requirements risk losing their active status.

A Realistic Plan for a New Channel

Most channels take a year or more to reach the Partner Program, and many never get there. The creators who make it usually pick a clear niche and commit to consistent, useful long-form videos rather than chasing whatever is trending.

A practical path looks like this. Choose a niche where you have real knowledge and where viewers make purchasing decisions, since that supports both higher RPMs and sponsorship potential. Publish long-form videos on a regular schedule and use Shorts to bring new viewers to them. Build an email list early, so your audience isn't entirely dependent on the algorithm. Once you reach 500 subscribers, turn on memberships and Shopping features. Keep a close eye on the February 2027 deadline if you're approaching the ad revenue threshold.

Ad revenue is a nice milestone, but it's rarely where a sustainable YouTube income comes from. The channels that last treat YouTube as the place their audience finds them, then earn from the trust that audience builds over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many subscribers do you need to make money on YouTube in 2026?

You can start earning through fan funding features such as memberships and Super Thanks at 500 subscribers, along with 3,000 public watch hours in the past year or 3 million Shorts views in the past 90 days. Ad revenue sharing currently requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in the past year or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. From February 1, 2027, new applicants for ad revenue will need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views instead.

How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views?

It depends mostly on niche and audience location. Creators in finance, business, and technology commonly report RPMs in the $10 to $30 range for long-form videos, while entertainment and gaming channels often see $1 to $5. Shorts pay far less per view. RPM is the creator's actual earnings per 1,000 views after YouTube's share, which is different from the CPM advertisers pay.

Will the 2027 YouTube Partner Program changes affect channels already monetized?

YouTube says channels already in the Partner Program are not affected by the higher entry thresholds. However, starting February 1, 2027, earning ad and subscription revenue from Shorts requires 10 million qualified Shorts views over the previous 90 days, and channels need to meet new activity requirements, such as 1,000 watch hours in the past year or regular uploads, to stay active in the program.
