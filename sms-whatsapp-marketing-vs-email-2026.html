SMS and WhatsApp Marketing vs Email in 2026: Which Channel Wins for E-commerce Revenue?

By Marcus Reid8 min readPublished September 14, 2026

Every online store eventually faces the same question. Email has carried the business for years, but text messages get read within minutes, and customers in many countries would rather talk to a brand on WhatsApp than open an inbox. Should you move budget into messaging apps, run all three channels, or stay focused on email?

The honest answer depends on what you're sending, who you're sending it to, and where they live. Each channel has very different costs, rules, and strengths. This guide lays them out side by side, including a change that trips up a lot of US stores: WhatsApp marketing messages currently can't be delivered to American phone numbers at all.

Headline stats like these need context. SMS open rates are high partly because a text notification is hard to ignore, which is different from every message persuading someone. Email's return figures come from averages that include mature, well-segmented programs. And the cost per message matters enormously once you multiply it by a list of 50,000 people several times a month. The more useful comparison is what each channel does best.

Where Email Still Wins

Email remains the cheapest way to reach a large list repeatedly. Most platforms charge by contact count rather than per message, so sending one more campaign costs almost nothing. That economics makes email the natural home for anything that happens often or needs space: newsletters, new collections, buying guides, post-purchase education, and longer automated sequences.

Email also carries more content. You can show several products, tell a brand story, include reviews, and let people browse. A text message that tried to do all of that would annoy people. And subscribers accept a higher sending frequency by email, since an unwanted email is easy to skim past while an unwanted text interrupts someone's day.

Where email falls short is speed and attention. Inboxes are crowded, promotions tabs hide campaigns, and many people check email only a few times a day. For a flash sale that ends in three hours, that delay is a real cost. Deliverability also requires ongoing care, which we cover in our guide to staying out of the spam folder.

Where SMS Wins (and What It Costs)

Text messages get seen quickly. That makes SMS well suited to moments where timing drives revenue: abandoned cart reminders sent within an hour, back-in-stock alerts for popular items, last-chance sale notices, and shipping updates. Omnisend's SMS research shows e-commerce brands sending far more texts each year, which reflects how well these short, urgent messages tend to perform.

The trade-offs are real, though:

  • Cost per message. Most SMS platforms charge per text, and MMS with images costs more. A campaign to a large list can cost more than a month of email.
  • Low tolerance. Subscribers unsubscribe from texts much faster than from emails when frequency climbs. Many stores find that a few promotional texts per month is the upper limit before opt-outs rise.
  • Limited space. A text has room for one idea and one link. It can't carry a catalog.
  • Strict legal requirements. US texting rules are far tighter than email rules, and mistakes can be expensive. More on that below.

The best SMS programs treat the channel as a premium tier. Subscribers who opt in to texts are often a store's most engaged customers, so a small, well-timed set of messages to them can be worth more than frequent blasts to everyone.

WhatsApp: Huge Outside the US, Restricted Inside It

WhatsApp has more than 3 billion monthly users, and in much of Latin America, Europe, India, Southeast Asia, and Africa it is the default way people message each other and businesses. For stores selling in those markets, WhatsApp can outperform both email and SMS, because customers already live in the app and can reply, ask questions, and complete orders in one conversation.

Businesses send WhatsApp messages at scale through the WhatsApp Business Platform, usually via a provider such as Twilio, Klaviyo, or a specialist tool. Since July 2025, Meta has charged per delivered template message, with prices that depend on the message category and the recipient's country. Marketing templates cost the most. Utility messages such as order updates cost less, and replies inside a 24-hour window after a customer messages you are free.

US stores need to know one major exception. Meta paused marketing template messages to US phone numbers starting April 1, 2025, and the pause was still in place in mid-2026 with no end date announced. You can still use WhatsApp with American customers for order confirmations, shipping notices, and customer service conversations they start, including those that begin from click-to-WhatsApp ads. You can't send promotional campaigns to US numbers through the platform.

Selling internationally? Segment your list by country before choosing a channel. The same promotion might go by WhatsApp to customers in Brazil or Spain, by SMS to customers in the US, and by email everywhere. A one-size-fits-all channel plan leaves money on the table in both directions.

The Compliance Rules That Can Sink a Text Program

Email marketing in the US mostly runs under CAN-SPAM, which allows sending to people who haven't explicitly opted in as long as you honor unsubscribes. Texting is completely different, and the penalties are steep.

  • Prior express written consent. The Telephone Consumer Protection Act requires clear, documented consent before any marketing text. A buried line in your terms of service isn't enough. Use an unchecked checkbox or a keyword opt-in that explains what people are signing up for and how often you'll text.
  • Opt-out handling. STOP and similar replies must be honored promptly, and help keywords should return contact details.
  • Quiet hours. Federal rules restrict telemarketing messages to between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time, and several states set tighter windows.
  • A2P 10DLC registration. US carriers require businesses to register their brand and messaging campaign before sending from standard 10-digit numbers. Unregistered traffic gets filtered or blocked.
  • State laws. A growing list of states, including Florida and Texas, have their own telemarketing rules that apply to texts. Texas's SB 140 took effect in September 2025 and brought texts clearly under its telemarketing law.

TCPA violations can cost $500 per message, and up to $1,500 when willful, which is why class action suits over marketing texts are common. The FCC's telemarketing guidance is a good starting point, and Infobip's TCPA guide walks through the practical setup. For a larger program, a short review with a lawyer who knows telecom rules is money well spent. For WhatsApp in Europe, GDPR consent rules apply in the same way they apply to email.

How to Split Messages Across All Three Channels

The stores that do best with messaging rarely pick one channel. They give each channel a job based on urgency, cost, and length. A simple starting split looks like this:

  • Welcome series: email for the full sequence, with one short SMS for subscribers who opted in to texts.
  • Abandoned cart: email first within an hour, then an SMS reminder for high-value carts if the email isn't opened.
  • Order and shipping updates: SMS or WhatsApp, depending on the customer's country and preference, with email as a record.
  • Flash sales and limited drops: SMS to engaged subscribers, email to the full list, WhatsApp to opted-in customers outside the US.
  • Newsletters, guides, and new collections: email only.
  • Customer questions: WhatsApp or SMS conversations, where customers can reply naturally.

Coordinate the channels so the same person doesn't get the same promotion three times in an afternoon. Most major e-commerce messaging platforms can suppress an SMS if the related email was already clicked, and that one setting prevents a lot of opt-outs.

Where to Start

If your email program is still basic, fix that first. Build the core automated flows, clean up your list, and get deliverability right. Email remains the cheapest channel to scale, and our guide to email automation workflows covers the sequences worth building.

Once email is working, add SMS with a consent-first opt-in at checkout and on your site, and start with just two automations: abandoned cart and back-in-stock. Measure revenue per message and opt-out rate before adding campaigns. If a meaningful share of your customers are outside the US, test WhatsApp for order updates and customer service in those markets, then expand into marketing messages where the rules allow it.

The goal isn't to send more messages. It's to send each message through the channel where the customer is most likely to welcome it. Get that right, and each channel makes the others more effective instead of competing for the same attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SMS marketing better than email for e-commerce?

Neither is better across the board. SMS gets read faster and works well for short, time-sensitive messages such as cart reminders and flash sales, but each message costs more and subscribers tolerate far fewer of them. Email is cheaper per send and better for longer content, product education, and regular campaigns. Most stores earn the most by using both, with email as the main channel and SMS reserved for high-intent moments.

Can US businesses send WhatsApp marketing messages?

Not to US phone numbers at the moment. Meta paused delivery of marketing template messages to US numbers on April 1, 2025, and had not announced an end date as of mid-2026. Utility messages like order and shipping updates, authentication codes, and replies inside the 24-hour customer service window still work, so WhatsApp remains useful for customer service and for stores selling to customers in other countries.

What consent do I need to send marketing texts in the US?

Under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act you need prior express written consent before sending marketing text messages, typically collected through a checkbox or keyword opt-in that clearly explains what the subscriber is agreeing to. You must honor STOP requests promptly, send only during permitted hours, and register your business and messaging campaign for A2P 10DLC if you use standard 10-digit numbers.
